**COTABATO CITY, Philippines** --- As expected, Abdulrauf Macacua was nominated as chief minister of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao during the inaugural session on Friday, October 30, of the newly-elected members of the 80-seat BARMM parliament.

Up to 51 BARMM parliament members voted for Macacua after he was nominated as chief minister at the onset of their session.

Macacua was the interim chief minister of BARMM, based on an appointment by President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr., from March 2025 until his election as parliament member during the September 14 regional elections.

Macacua defeated, in the selection for chief minister by parliament members, a lone contender, Ahod Ebrahim, whose bid for the post was supported by only 27 regional lawmakers.

Ebrahim was BARMM’s chief minister from 2019, after the creation of BARMM then via a plebiscite, until 2025.

Ebrahim is the chairman of the Moro Islamic Liberation Front, whose 2014 peace pact with the national government paved the way for the creation of BARMM in 2019, replacing the then 27-year now defunct, less empowered Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao.

“I shall do my best to serve the Muslim Christian and the non-Moro indigenous communities in the region,” Macacua said after his nomination as chief minister of the duly-mandated parliament during their session on Friday.

President Marcos attended the opening program of Friday’s symbolic first session of the duly-elected BARMM parliament members, held at the region’s capitol in uptown Cotabato City.

Like Macacua, all other members of the interim BARMM parliament, who served prior to the September 14 regional electoral exercise, were presidential appointees.

In a brief talk with parliament members before their inaugural session, President Marcos assured of his continuing support for the peace and development efforts of the BARMM government.

“Peace is what we can offer to each other,” the President told members of the BARMM parliament.

The President also urged them to work cohesively in promoting peace throughout the autonomous region, which has five provinces and three cities.

Macacua said he is thankful to the 51 lawmakers who chose him to stay as figurehead of the parliament. 

All 51 lawmakers also voted for a nominee for parliament speaker, Amer Zacaria Rakim. Only 27 members of the BARMM parliament supported another nominee for the post, Mohammad Yacob.
