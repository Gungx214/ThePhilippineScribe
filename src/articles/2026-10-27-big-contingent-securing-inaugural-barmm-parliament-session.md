**COTABATO CITY, Philippines** --- The police and military have deployed hundreds of troops, backed by combat vehicles, in this city to ensure the safety of the inaugural session on Friday, October 30, of the 80 newly-elected members of the Bangsamoro parliament.

Army and police officials separately said on Tuesday that the tight security set-up shall continue even after the event to ensure peace and calm in all of the 37 barangays in the city, as requested by residents and local business and peace-advocacy organizations.  

The regional lawmakers to converge on Friday, 46 of them now in a multi-party bloc identified with the region’s interim chief minister, Abdulraof Macacua, are expected to elect by then their figurehead, who shall, in concurrent capacity, also oversee all of the ministries and support agencies in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao.

The term of Macacua as an interim parliament member and as regional chief minister, based on an appointment by President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr., will end on Friday. He was elected representative of a parliamentary seat covering neighboring barangays in Cotabato City and in Maguindanao del Norte province during BARMM’s September 14 regional elections.

Like Macacua, Ahod Ebrahim, the chairman of the Moro Islamic Liberation Front who had served as interim chief minister of BARMM from 2019 to 2025, also got elected as parliament member during the September 14 elections. His supporters want him to get reinstated as BARMM chief minister of the now duly-mandated, regular parliament. 

All of the five governors in BARMM, Tucao Mastura of Maguindanao del Norte, Ali Midtimbang of Maguindanao del Sur, Mamintal Adiong, Jr. of Lanao del Sur, Mujiv Hataman of Basilan and Ysmael Sali of Tawi-Tawi, together manifested their desire for Macacua, president of the Bangsamoro Federalist Party, to stay as regional chief minister during a gathering in Davao City just two weeks ago.

More than half of the 116 town mayors in BARMM and the local executives in its three component-cities, Lamitan, Marawi and Basilan, are members of the Bangsamoro Federalist Party. 

“We have enough soldiers scattered around Cotabato City as part of our security efforts related to the October 30 inaugural session of the now regular, duly mandated Bangsamoro regional parliament,” Brig. Gen. Edgar Catu, commander of the Army’s 6th Infantry Division, told reporters on Tuesday, while inspecting the Army and police personnel guarding the routes connecting Cotabato City to nearby towns in Maguindanao del Norte province.

The 6th ID, units of the Cotabato City Police Office, led by its director, Col. Jibin Bongcayao, the Police Regional Office-Bangsamoro Autonomous Region and the 6th Marine Rifle Battalion of the 1st Marine Brigade are cooperating in ensuring the safety of the October 30 inaugural session of the Bangsamoro parliament. 

Policemen led by Bongcayao and from units of PRO-BAR, under Police Brig. Gen. Christopher Abecia, are now visible along busy thoroughfares in Cotabato City, the capital of BARMM, where its regional government center is located.

“We are not taking chances. We will do our best to make October 30 and the days after peaceful, safe for all,” Bongcayao said.
